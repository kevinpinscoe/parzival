# Why did I call it the Parzival?

| Fisher King motif         | Credential/workflow analogy                                                                    |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The wounded king          | The damaged or poorly understood authentication system                                         |
| The barren kingdom        | Workflows and shell scripts that cannot operate reliably                                       |
| The concealed wound       | An undocumented credential source, permission problem, or broken injection mechanism           |
| The Grail                 | The protected credential or authority needed to perform the work                               |
| The ritual procession     | Credentials being passed automatically through pipelines without anyone understanding how      |
| Parzival's failure to ask | Engineers accepting opaque automation instead of investigating its authority                   |
| The healing question      | "Which identity is running this, where does its credential come from, and what can it access?" |

The metaphor can be summarized as:

Parzival protects the Grail by examining how credentials move through automated workflows. When a credential is exposed, the Fisher King is wounded and the operational landscape suffers. By asking the right questions and preventing the leak, Parzival enables the wound to be healed and the system restored.
